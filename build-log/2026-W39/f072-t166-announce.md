### F072 · T166 — Announce something, with a time and a place on it

**The word is announcement.** Not bulletin, which #176 retired, and not post, which is what the table is called and what nobody says out loud. Every user-facing string on this surface says announcement or announce. The substrate keeps its own names — `page_posts`, `group.post_create` — because renaming a table is not a copy change, and a migration in this ticket would have been scope nobody asked for.

**The deferral is discharged.** `group.post_create` inserted six columns and deliberately skipped `starts_at`, with a comment deferring to F073. F072 absorbed F073 on 2026-09-21. Both `starts_at` and `location_id` now go through the write path, on create and on edit. **No migration**: the columns and their indexes have existed since T162, and `browse_feed` already compares both window bounds against `starts_at` without truncating to a day, and already drops past-dated announcements on its own.

**The hard part was not the column, it was what time seven o'clock is.** `timestamptz` normalises to UTC and discards the offset it was written with, so nothing in the row remembers that seven meant seven in Sacramento. Rendering in the reader's browser zone shows a Sacramento evening as a New York night. `src/lib/metro/metro-time.ts` is the one place the zone exists, with **#173 named in it** — when the metro gets its own column, that file changes and nothing else does. The conversion is **two-pass**, because the offset depends on the answer: guess with the offset at the wall-clock reading, then re-read the offset at the instant that guess produced. One pass is wrong by exactly an hour across a DST boundary, which is the failure that shows up twice a year and never in a test written in June. Both 2026 boundaries are tested, as are the clock readings that never happened and the one that happened twice.

**Half a time is refused rather than completed.** F072 § Not this rules out all-day announcements, so a date on its own has nothing to become — and guessing midnight would put a Thursday evening on Wednesday night for a reader one zone east. The composer says "Add both a date and a time, or leave them both empty."

**The address is the announcement's own**, through the same `<LocationPlaceFields>` the Page edit form and creation use. **The Location is made before the announcement is written**, so a place that cannot be made leaves nothing behind — criterion 5, held at the point where it could actually be broken rather than only in the handler's transaction.

**The audience switch uses the ratified copy verbatim** and nothing here chose any of it. Label *Who sees this*; on and default *Anyone*; off *Only people who get updates from you*; beneath it *42 right now*, or *Nobody yet* at zero — never `0`, per design-language principle 11. **The words name the people, never a kind of post**, which reads as a contradiction of "announcement is the word everywhere" and is not: the word belongs to the primary control, and this switch is about reach.

**The restricted setting is visible, choosable, and not postable, and says why.** Follower delivery does not exist (`ROADMAP.md` § Cut). Telling a creator they reached forty-two people none of whom receive anything is a lie; hiding the setting is a different lie, that reaching your own followers was never the idea. Choosing it disables Announce and shows the reason in one sentence. **The count is a count and never a roster** (Don, 2026-09-07) — `countPageFollowers` uses `head: true`, so the read cannot return a row even if someone later wants one.

**One conformance trap, caught by the check that exists for it.** The edit's SET clause is built at runtime, and the first version interpolated it into the query template — Rule 4's whole subject. Rebuilt on the closed-literal pattern `group.update` already uses (`PostSetClause` + the `sql-injection-safe: enum-constrained by` annotation). It surfaced as 13 red tests across seven files, all of them one cause.

Tests: 54 new (the composer's 24, the timezone helper's 15 including both DST boundaries, the write path's, and the resolver's two new fields). Two mutations were planted to confirm the composer's tests bite — renaming the button and swapping the zone conversion for a naive one — and both went red.

**Verified in the running app, not only in tests.** Against local Postgres with the seeded Pages: the canonical address renders, `/p/<place>/g/<slug>`, a stale slug, an upper-case address and `/manage/<slug>` all 308 to canonical, an unknown id 404s, and a dated announcement renders as *"Thursday, September 24 at 7:00pm · Orchard Hill church hall"* with its undated sibling beside it carrying no time at all. The insert and the dynamic edit were both run against Postgres directly, because a wrong placeholder count passes a mocked unit test and fails in production.

**Not done here, and not hidden:** the signed-in owner's own view of the composer was not driven in a browser — local sign-in needs an account this session should not create. It is covered by the 24 component tests and by the write path against a real database.

**No migration.**
